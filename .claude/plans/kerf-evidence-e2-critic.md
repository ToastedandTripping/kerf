### 2. Approach soundness — **PASS**
Pure builders consumed by both dry-run and live paths, a pure `preflight`, one `cleanup`, a scripted fake behind a lazy `open_serial`: the right shape, and the reason the twenty ids are single-site. One geometry inconsistency: `P(u, v)` scales by `box/60`, so the M4 cases' "20 mm segment" from (5,5) to (25,5) is `20·box/60` mm — 10 mm at the `--box-mm 30` floor the plan permits. Either the M4 offsets are unscaled millimetres (then say so and check `origin + 25 ≤ bed`) or the "≥ 30" floor and "20 mm" are both wrong. This matters because the segment length is what the hold has to land inside (X3 below).

### 3. Completeness — **CONCERN**
(a) **Wedge-case preamble unstated.** Today `main` sends `G21`, `G90`, `M5` before the variants (`:487-489`); the plan's wedge case is "the eight existing sequences unchanged" and the general sentence "every case emits `G21` first". `G90` must be in the wedge builder explicitly (and asserted by a test), because the sequences are absolute `G0 X40 Y40` lines and a `G91` left by an interrupted `direction` run on a fork whose reset does not restore parser defaults would turn them into 40 mm relative moves off the box. (b) **What RESULT does a designed `0x18` yield?** `hold-m4` and `stop-m4` end with a case-planned `0x18`. If that goes through `cleanup`, every such run ends `RESULT INCOMPLETE` by construction and the card's rule discards it; if it is a `Step`, the run ends `COMPLETE` after its capture. The plan does not say. Related: with the default `--reps 5`, a fully successful `hold-m4` ends `INCOMPLETE: controller not Idle before rep 2` — the plan knows this ("effectively one repetition") but leaves the default in place, and astra 3.2 step 8 passes `--reps 3` to `stop-m4`. Refuse `--reps > 1` for those two cases at argparse time (exit 2) and name the departure from astra 3.2. (c) **Feed for the M4 cases.** `--feed` defaults to 12000. A 20 mm `G1` at F12000 finishes in about 0.1 s plus acceleration; `!` after `HOLD_DELAY_S` and `0x18` after `STOP_DELAY_S` land after motion has ended, so `hold-m4` observes an Idle controller and `stop-m4` "stops" nothing. The old pause experiment crawled at F300 over 70 mm for exactly this reason (`:395-396`), and astra 3.2 steps 8-9 pass `--feed 300`. The plan's card is silent on feed. Fix: the three M4 cases refuse in preflight unless `segment_mm / (feed/60) > HOLD_DELAY_S + 1` (pure, one mutant), and the card writes `--feed 300` into each M4 command line; astra 3.2's "then admitted job feed" for hold/stop is unrealisable inside a 60 mm box and should be named as not satisfied. (d) **`cleanup` before the wire exists / when the port dies.** If `open_serial` raises, or Ctrl-C lands during the banner wait, there is no `wire` for `cleanup(wire, reason)`; and if the port is gone (`__EOF__` → `Wedge("port closed")`), `send_byte(0x18)` raises inside cleanup step 1. Both must still write `RESULT INCOMPLETE: <reason>` and exit non-zero (steps 2-3 under `finally`). (e) **The `Alarm` refusal has no operator path.** See X1 — the card says "clear it yourself" without saying with what, and the probe's own startup can put the controller into that state. (f) **Position after `hold-m4`/`stop-m4`.** DECISIONS 2026-09-20 records ~10 mm displacement from hold-then-reset, and GRBL's abort-during-cycle alarm text says position is not guaranteed. The card must require re-homing before the next boxed case; the plan's "the controller may be in Alarm" is not that instruction. (g) Module docstring `:3-25` describes the old CLI (`--pause`, "default 10", auto-picked port); the plan does not list it among what changes. (h) `$#`: the plan refuses on a non-zero `G54` and ignores `G92`, which shifts the frame identically. Add `G92` to the refusal (or `G54` in the preamble plus `G92` refused).

1. **The owner ran the probe and it refused every time with "controller in Alarm".** The fork locks after the soft reset the probe sends at startup; the card said "clear it yourself"; `$X` from a terminal was undone by the next start's reset. ### Inversion — when would a rejected alternative win?
- **Keeping an explicit `--home`** wins if the operator has no other way to present a homed, Idle controller to a tool that resets on start. On this board that condition is true today (the startup `0x18` is the normal path, claim 18 note). Astra excludes automatic homing, not operator-selected homing.
- **Patching the old script instead of rewriting** wins if fewer than six call sites needed the cleanup change. There are six, plus the startup sequence and the CLI; the rewrite is right.
- **`--allow-constant-power`** wins if any planned case needs `$32=0`. None does; Lee's 2026-09-10 ruling on the app's equivalent stands; correctly rejected.
- **A header rewritten to INCOMPLETE** (the parent's wording) wins if a crashed process could rewrite its header. It cannot; the last-line rule is right, once the join makes the last line deterministic.

## Overall verdict

**CONCERN.** The plan does what the parent and both rulings asked: the byte that re-arms the beam is gone with a named refusal, direction and origin come from the operator, every powered case is gated on a `$32=1` readback with no override, cleanup is realtime-first on every path including the one line the parent missed, and the evidence discipline is status-only end to end. Every citation into the script is exact and the battery mechanics are as stated. What it leaves unowned is the machine's coordinate frame: it removed the startup home and unlock without giving the operator a way to present a homed Idle controller to a tool whose own startup resets the board every run on this hardware, and nothing checks the frame the bounds test assumes. Three smaller defects turn good captures into bad evidence: the RESULT line can be overtaken by the reader thread, the M4 cases at the default feed finish before the hold or stop is sent, and a designed reset has no stated RESULT. None needs a redesign; all are one paragraph each in the plan and one seam each in the script.
